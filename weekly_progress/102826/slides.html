1The system we study · an agent harness (our s15)

A harness loops model calls over a prompt it keeps editing

  • Benchmark runs use a slim harness: lead, subagent and task board only; there the rolling snip makes up 99.9% of all edited prompt text
  • Why it matters to the server: between edits the prompt only grows at its end, so cached KV is reused (freezing the system prompt, your Oct 9 change, lifted reuse from 4.2% to 89.3%); every edit forces a recompute of everything after it

Sources: s15_integrated_harness/code.py (agent_loop, prepare_context, snip_compact, micro_compact, fit_tool_results, compact_history, spawn_subagent, the task board, MessageBus); research/method_develop/README.md (the snip's share of edited text, from 85,527 recorded transitions).

9backup B6 · what we concluded

What holds, what is offline only, what is open, what we dropped

Established

measured on every run, or pre-registered
  • Fix the harness before measuring a cache: a per-second timestamp held KV reuse at 4.2%; a frozen system prompt and an append-only template give 89.3%
  • The rolling snip is the one frequent edit: 99.9% of edited text in benchmark runs; it cuts cache hits from 61–92% to 9–54%
  • KV repair is safe for accuracy: shift + EPIC-128 costs under 5 points (pre-registered, 100 tasks × 3) and computes 91% fewer prompt tokens; plain shift loops more
  • Drift grows with every shift (reply KL 0.0006 → 0.0165) and EPIC repairs only 11–30% of it; accuracy does not need the rest
  • With many users, GPU memory binds, not compute, CPU or links: past 16 users hits fall to 1–2%; a session gate restores 3.5–3.8×; KV reads also take 38–49% of a decode step
  • Tools are cheap here (mean 12.4 ms); a cost hint steers the model only between equivalent tools
  • Teams copy shared files: (N−1)/N of the bytes; the lead re-reads what compaction dropped (82%); only sizing the budget fixes the lead without a quality cost
  • The lead's task graph is exact (45/45 edges); passing predecessors' bytes cuts rounds 5.17 → 3.00, but read rounds are a minority (42.4%)

Offline only

from logs, replays or models
  • Edits are material and predictable: ≈30% of GPU time at 4–8 users, 99.5% known a round ahead
  • The waste is known text: ~95% of prefill below the turning point was computed before; dead blocks hold ≥ 19–24% of the pool
  • Decodes stall behind prefill for 21–45% of a call at 4–8 users
  • A DRAM tier would serve 54% → 86% of prefixes at 32 users (simulated, 22 points optimistic)
  • Retention rules: key on content ranges, spend the budget on prediction, widen the pool, do not partition per agent

Not yet established

the next experiments
  • The payoff with many users filling GPU memory: emulated reuse computes every token, so it needs a real reuse path
  • Margins under 5 points: 2 points at 80% power needs about 6× e3's 300 blocks
  • Terminal-Bench accuracy: its round-trip check is 95.7–96.6%, below 99%, until the harness keeps token-level history
  • Hybrid and compressed-KV models (Qwen3.8, DeepSeek); the design's scheduling and residency claims; the P/D crossover; live closure inheritance
  • Inferred only: FP8 KV fitting about 2× the users

Retired, parked or withdrawn

kept out of every slide (details: B7)
  • Retired: exact background refresh (49% of its value at 0.32 of the tokens); graph-directed retention; “evict what is simplest to recover”; lm-eval as a task source; KV splice and the context interventions; scheduling knobs against thrashing
  • Parked: divergence triggers JSD, TVD, EXP (noise floor 66–72% of the signal; batch-invariant probes cost 2.6× decode); live DeepSeek reuse
  • Dropped: Qwen3-32B as the study model (pass@1 0.02–0.09); GAIA
  • Withdrawn: every single-session time conclusion (job-time ratios, “decode dominates”, throughput ceilings, “P/D and KV reuse do not pay”); “no arm differs detectably”; e1/e2's age-cap drop; “tools take 30–85%” here; the early prefill fit (~8× too high)

Sources: weekly_progress/102126/slides.html slide 8 and conclusion.md, 091626/tiered_memory_conclusion.md, 092326/conclusion.md, the 090926, 093026 and 100726 summary slides, and the topic READMEs.
