7II · A The harness system · an overview

Each user's harness works a task graph with a lead and teammates; every agent's call re-sends its whole context to one shared vLLM backend

  • Teams multiply sessions: a lead with k teammates is 1 + k sessions on the same backend

9II · C Methods · what could relieve the bottleneck

Six methods could relieve the HBM bottleneck: four accepted serving techniques, and two that need what only the harness knows

1KV cache reuse across tiers
accepted · skips prefill
  • Is the prefix cached? A hit skips its prefill; decode needs the KV in the decoding GPU's HBM
  • On which HBM? This GPU's: a hit. Under DP keep sessions sticky; under P/D the decode GPU pulls the blocks it lacks
  • In DRAM, to which HBM? The decoding GPU's: 0.31 s for 23.5k tokens (RTX), ~0.6 s on a loaded H200
  • On flash? ~1.5–2 s to read (derived); slow writes keep it to idle sessions. Recompute: 3.3–6.1 s
Result 3: a DRAM tier gives 1.65–3×
2P/D disaggregation
accepted · separates the phases
  • What P:D ratio? One TP=1 prefill GPU serves ~6.5k lead calls/h, one decode GPU ~10k: this workload balances near 3 P : 2 D (estimate)
  • Why split? In vLLM a decode that shares an engine step with a prefill chunk waits for the whole chunk, up to ~1.2 s: 21–45% of a 32B call at 4–8 sessions; P/D removes that wait
  • Price: a KV transfer per call; 14's sweep (queued) measures where it pays
→ slide 10
3Serving layouts: TP, DP, PP
accepted · splits the model
  • TP splits weights and KV across GPUs: one larger pool (TP=2: 777k tokens, 3× one GPU's) and 1.7–1.9× faster calls; NVLink stays ≤ 5%
  • DP runs whole copies with separate pools: a session's KV lives on one copy, so keep sessions sticky
  • PP runs the layers in stages: TP's capacity without its per-call speed-up (not measured)
12 §3.5–§3.6: TP=1 and TP=2 measured
4Prefill time from the new tokens
accepted · prices a call
  • prefill(n new after c cached) ≈ 96.9 µs·n + 3.46 ns·n·(c + n/2) + 12.6 ms per chunk (11's step model, 2.03M steps, R² = 1.000)
  • For example: a 232-token append 0.05 s; a 12.6k-token edit after 7.9k cached 1.86 s; the whole 23.1k context 3.20 s
  • Use: price a call on arrival: restore or recompute, route long prefills, order the queue
5Request type
agent-specific · the harness knows it
  • Resume or modify? 77–86% of follow-up calls append to the cached history (a median 124–433 new tokens); 14–23% edit it and re-prefill 12–16k tokens
  • On or off the critical path? The lead's next call keeps its user waiting; memory extraction (async since 09) and background summaries can wait
  • Policy (proposed): hold a resume's cached prefix until it is admitted; run off-path work below every lead call
→ slide 11
6Dead blocks first
agent-specific · the harness knows it
  • Dead blocks: after a modify, the replaced history's KV can never be hit again: 15–21k cached tokens per edit, 73–96% of evictions below the turning point
  • Evict them first: vLLM's LRU cannot tell dead from idle; the harness knows which version it replaced
  • In simulation: it helps while the pool has slack; in a 128 GiB DRAM tier at 32 sessions, 54% → 86% served
→ slide 12

Blue: accepted; orange: agent-specific. Sources: 13 RESULTS §3, S0_ceilings.md; 12 §3.5–§3.6; 14 S0_predictions.md; 11 §2.6; 15 §4.1, §4.6; 10 §4.2.
