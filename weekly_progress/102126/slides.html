5I · E e4: recompute when the KV has drifted?

D_JSD measures how far the repaired KV has drifted from the original; as a recompute trigger it tracks the real error but sits near its own noise floor

Setupepic128rjsd: full recompute with probability JSDfirst-token distributions, top-1286,414 decisions, 0 failed

How D_JSD is computed and used, at every edited call

DJSD(O, E) = √( ½ KL(O ‖ M) + ½ KL(E ‖ M) ),  M = ½ (O + E), log base 2 → 0 when E predicts what O does, 1 when they share nothing
  • O and E: two 1-token forwards (temperature 0, top-128 log-probs) give the first-token distribution under the original KV, every reused row in its first-ever version moved to its new position, and under the after-EPIC KV this call would serve. KL, TVD and 1 − e−KL are logged too
  • Decision: u comes from a hash of the task, replicate and call, uniform in [0, 1). If u < DJSD, everything after the never-edited head is recomputed fresh and becomes the new original; otherwise the repaired KV is served. So the recompute probability equals DJSD
  • Cost: the original-KV probe's tokens at every decision, plus the after-EPIC prefill when it recomputes

8Conclusion

Approximate KV repair is safe for agent accuracy; whether it pays is decided under contention, and that is where the paper has to be won

Established

1
Fix the harness before measuring a cache method: one timestamp held reuse at 4.2%; static prompt + append-only template give 89.3%
2
EPIC-128 after the snip costs under 5 points (pre-registered, 100 tasks × 3) and computes 91% fewer prompt tokens; MiniMax does not loop under it
3
Edits are predictable and material under load: 99.5% known a round ahead; about 30% of GPU time at 4–8 sessions (offline, first order)

Retired or parked

×
Drift-bounded exact refresh: neither pays nor fits (D2, D3)
×
Divergence triggers (JSD, TVD, EXP): the floor is 66–72% of the signal; parked until probes are batch-invariant (2.6× decode today)
×
Qwen3-32B as the study model (pass@1 0.022–0.089), GAIA (dropped 10-12; its search key returned 403), live DeepSeek reuse

Not established

  • The payoff with many users, when prefill and cache management compete for the whole HBM: emulation computes every token, so it needs a real reuse path
  • Margins under 5 points: 2 points at 80% power needs about 6× e3's 300 blocks
  • Terminal-Bench accuracy: its round-trip check stays below 99% (95.7–96.6%) until the harness keeps token-level history
  • Hybrid (Qwen3.8) and compressed-KV (DeepSeek) models; the design's scheduling and residency claims (C1, C3)
The paper, as it stands
  • Claim: an agent harness edits its context predictably (99.5% a round ahead), and cheap approximate KV repair is safe for accuracy (pre-registered), so a server can schedule edits as repair work and manage KV versions under contention
  • Missing: the payoff under contention, and a real (not emulated) reuse path
  • Go / no-go: the E1 simulator shows at least 10% over the best baseline

KV management for hybrid models (Qwen3.8-27B)

Why it is open (harness_eval)
  • Two kinds of layer: 16 × (3 Gated DeltaNet + 1 gated attention). The 16 attention layers keep 64 KiB of KV per token, movable by re-rotation (RoPE on 64 of 256 dims)
  • The 48 DeltaNet layers keep a state, not KV: ~72 MiB per sequence (the size of 1,152 tokens of attention KV), summing up every earlier token. After a snip every later token must run through the whole model again, so reuse saved no compute: the arms computed what full re-prefill does (66.5% of the prompt), not the 5.7–6.1% the attention layers alone need
  • Stale attention KV still drifts: with exact DeltaNet states, first-token KL rose from 0.007 before the first snip to 0.032–0.035 after 20 or more; in a 5-task pilot, EPIC runs took 118–128 lead calls against 78

12backup B3 · P:D control, the deep dive

In a harness the waiting queue is nearly empty below the turning point, so a P:D controller needs the harness's one-round forecast

One harness session's cycle

  • The queue is the wrong input below the turning point: a lead call waits 0.01 s in it on average; the demand a P:D role flip must prepare for is still in the sessions, decoding or in a tool. Above it the queue is real: 3.2 s per call at 12 users, 19.0 at 16, 75.3 at 32 (093026)
  • Flip ahead of the burst: a role flip waits about one round for decodes to drain; the harness forecast has about one round of lead
  • Match each cause of prefill to its resource: new tokens need prefill GPUs; edits need repair or a harness fix; eviction recompute needs residency (a session gate, a DRAM tier), not more prefill GPUs
  • Size for critical demand, then shape the rest through the harness

E0g, proposed (not run, not yet pre-registered)

rulepasses if
G1 lead time≥ 50% of prefill GPU-seconds have size and arrival known ≥ 5 s ahead from harness state
G2 forecastthe harness forecast's error is ≤ half the best queue-only forecaster's (5 s and 30 s windows)
G3 regretwith a one-round role-flip delay, ≥ 30% less capacity shortfall than the best queue-only controller
G4 causein topic 11's thrashing cells (12–32 sessions), add no prefill GPUs and recover throughput through the gate (3.5–3.8×)
