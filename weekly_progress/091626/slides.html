Method · a TTL priced in seconds, not a constant

  • τ* = argmaxτ P(τ,f)·(T·η + Prefill-Reload) − τ, over that tool's own observed durations. T is measured online — the queueing term is invisible to offline profiling, which is why no prior cost model carries it.
  • η = −Corr(k, N−k): continuity is worth exactly what remaining work is predictable. 1 fixed turn count, 0 geometric, negative for a long tail.
  • Program-level FCFS, pinned-first; deadlock broken by evicting the latest pin. ~1k lines on vLLM, ~1 ms overhead.

What it changes for us

  • Their per-turn queueing delay is the published name for our 3.7 s fixed round term, and for the ~80% of a hosted fault round that is queue plus TTFT floor.
  • Our tools cost 10–50 ms, 20–90× shorter than their 925 ms, so P(τ,f) ≈ 1 at tiny τ: retention should be near-unconditional on s15.
  • But they read tool duration off the inter-request interval, which on s15 also holds 13–21 s of memory_extract. Cost grows in τ while benefit is fixed, so harness turn-end work inverts their model — the gap next to steps 12 and 13.

Why it is the cost that shows

  • It runs after every lead turn, including the short turns that team events wake: 13-21 s each, 42-58% of the lead's busy time in team mode, 1,364 s of 3,946 s over the 24 runs.
  • It holds the agent lock, so the next turn waits for it; 35-74% of these calls in team mode hit the memory runtime's 1,000-token cap, the model still thinking when cut off.
  • Memory recall costs 4.9 s per call once records exist: the 7% of a CODE-lead round on slide 4.
  • Under context pressure the other model-driven cost appears. At the 50k-char limit preparation per round was 0.65 s, of which 0.62 s was one summary-compaction call amortised over the run and 25 ms the compaction code; at 512k the whole pipeline costs 2 ms.

Running the extraction once per user request, or asynchronously outside the lock, would cut team-run wall time by 28-48%. Preparation only costs when the harness calls the model to do it.

6Result · the price of one tool call (new, 2026-09-15)

Every tool costs 0.2-10 ms except four that scale with their arguments; the dearest, create_worktree at 607 ms, is one sixth of a call's 3.72 s fixed component

What was run

54 case labels (42 lead-side, 12 teammate-side) dispatched through the same code the agent loops use: trace span, permission hooks, handler, PostToolUse hook. The provider client is patched to raise, so no model call is spent. One warm-up plus 24 timed dispatches per case, shuffled, two independent runs: 2,556 timed dispatches, zero errors, about 15 s per run.

  • A flat floor of 0.5-3 ms across both pools. read_file ≈ 3.5 ms + 0.23 ms per KB returned, so a windowed read (limit=50, 2.9 ms) is 2-12x cheaper than the whole file at these sizes.
  • bash is the command: ls 8.7 ms, a scoped grep 9-11 ms, python3 on a unit-test file 135 ms.
  • Denial is 10-100x cheaper than execution (0.3-1.0 ms); a refused call costs the extra model round it triggers, never the refusal.
  • The teammate tax is permission, not machinery: identical operations land within a millisecond of the lead's (recursive glob 24.4 vs 23.1 ms, 13 KB read 7.9 vs 6.4 ms). What distinguishes a teammate is its 10-tool pool and the async rule that denies its non-display shell in 1.0 ms.
  • Scale: the median tool call, 1-3 ms, is three orders of magnitude below one model call; the worst realistic case, a 135 ms test run, is 3.6% of the fixed component.

A per-tool cost table is now measured rather than guessed: a lookup for the 22 flat tools, argument-aware pricing for bash, read_file, glob and create_worktree.

  • In a mixed pool the model picks on task fit and never consults the price: it raised the cost in 0-3% of its replies whatever the label said.
  • Between substitutes cost is a tie-break, not a budget. A 5x gap and a 7500x gap are avoided at the same rate and a tie returns it to chance, so published costs need only be in the right order, not accurate; an agent that respects them is not thereby scheduling well.

  • Labels are nearly free to send (about 230 tokens, all in the cached prefix) and cost most when used: replies grow by 18-31 tokens, a third to half a second of decode.

8Result · cross-teammate copies

Cross-teammate duplication equals (N−1)/N exactly: it measures how the tasks were cut, not the harness, and no context budget touches it

What the copies cost

  • When teammates share a file, 52-80% of the bytes they load are copies another teammate already holds; with disjoint corpora, 0%. In prefill-heavy runs the duplicates are 45-54% of all teammate prompt tokens (39-49% of the uncached ones); in decode-heavy runs the rate is as high but the volume small, 14-31%.
  • Teammates never compact, so the team ends a run holding 2-5 resident copies of the same file.
  • 94-100% of duplicates arrive within 60 s in a single wave; second waves re-fetch them 6-16 min later, past the provider cache's roughly 5-minute idle lifetime.

Raising the lead's budget 50k → 512k chars

  • Fixed the lead: zero summary compactions and zero placeholder rewrites in all eight runs, no report lost, no duplicate task or second wave, no timeout; wall 1.7-2.6× shorter where runs had hit the 1,200 s ceiling (PF-S 788 / 447 s, DC-S 673 / 518 s).
  • Left the teammates unchanged: 61-67% for shared corpora, 52.8% in all four PF-P runs, 0% disjoint. The lead's own cache hit rate fell to 4-23% because snip_compact still rewrites its history past 50 messages.

Source: research/02_input_redundancy/README.md Part B §2, §6 and §B.W (was teammate_input_redundancy.md §3 A-E, §5-6); research/05_latency_breakdown/README.md §A.W (was latency_breakdown.md §5; FQA and CODE rows; MATH read no files). PF-S run 1 at 50k spawned a fourth teammate 16 min in, hence N=4; DC-S run 2 at 50k spawned two extra after provider 429s. The provider's cache is strictly prefix-based (identical resend 99.9% cached, same body behind a new prefix 0%, a unique block mid-prompt 0%), so a shared block is reusable only at the same position in the same prefix: the constraint on next step 5.

10Next steps

Next: attack the eviction order rather than the prefill, and turn the task board into a retention policy the engine can act on

Two directions for next week

12
eviction order · the harness tier

The extra rounds come from the eviction order, so cache reuse should be judged on rounds removed, not prefill saved.

  • micro_compact keeps the three newest tool results and replaces the rest with a pointer, oldest first. For a pass over a corpus that is then revisited, oldest-first is the worst possible order: 77 to 100% of re-acquisitions hit a result that had already become a pointer.
  • Every miss buys a whole round: one model call carrying 3.7 s of fixed latency, a tool call, and a fresh prefill of bytes the context already held. Three replayed runs spent 39, 21 and 10 rounds this way. The prefill is the cheap part, under 2% of a call, so keeping the KV is worth it for the round that never happens, not for the FLOPs.
  • To run: replace oldest-first with a reuse-aware order, and keep the evicted block addressable so a recall costs neither a tool call nor a prefill. Score it on re-acquisition rounds, not on prefill tokens.
13
DAG-aware eviction · the engine tier

Nothing caps a request's share of HBM except capacity itself, so the harness should publish what its task graph already implies.

  • No engine rejects on capacity. LRU decides, and it evicts exactly the prefix an idle teammate will want next: 18.5% of a KV pool sits idle on tool calls and 40 to 60% of session time is paused.
  • The harness holds what the engine lacks. The task board is a dependency graph: which teammate is blocked and on what, which is about to be unblocked, which has finished, and how long each has been idle. Continuum pins a session's KV for a computed TTL (slide 1) and KVFlow evicts by steps-to-execution, but neither is driven by a task DAG the client already maintains.
  • To run: derive a per-session retention directive from the board, hold, demote or drop, and test it on the self-hosted vLLM where blocks can be pinned. Watch idle against active KV, and who is retracted when --max-num-seqs forces preemption.
