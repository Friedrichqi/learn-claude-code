Inheritance removes the rounds it can reach

Hand a successor task the bytes its predecessor already read, and it stops spending rounds fetching them. How to read it

What the values say

  • Frequency-aware beats recency, modestly: at 8 KB, GDSF 20.8% and LFU 19.2% against LRU's 15.8% on τ-bench/GAIA — random eviction gets 13.6% — and 2.6–2.9% against 0.3% on SWE-bench.
  • Prediction, not capacity, is the gap: foresight at 8 KB buys the whole unlimited value on replay (38.0 of 38.2), yet the best deployable policy gets 58% of it on τ-bench.
  • The previous task first leads τ-bench (dag's 12.7% again), but replay task order is arbitrary, so it says nothing about a graph; in our harness graph order stays within a point of LRU.
  • Our harness keeps whole files (median pool 75 KB), so 8 KB holds almost nothing; at 32 KB whole-round picking doubles LRU.

What the values say

  • The control flow is identical. Rounds per task, redundancy and quality match, so the 1.9–2.3× wall-time gap is decode speed times output length: rounds are 1.5–2.8× longer, not more numerous.
  • The memory tax is untouched: 12–35 s per Lead turn and 20–35% of run wall on both — the largest cost outside the agents' own calls, and worse with a thinking model, since the 1,000-token extraction call is mostly thinking. That is next step 18.
  • Output tokens are each provider's own; Qwen's tokenizer is 12–15% denser, so its outputs are 1.05–1.8× longer in characters.

Two vLLM facts a KV-sharing design must respect

  • Cache blocks are 784 tokens on this hybrid model (48 Gated-DeltaNet and 16 full-attention layers): a re-send is served from cache only in whole blocks.
  • A foreign request resumes only at a checkpoint. Under --mamba-cache-mode align recurrent state is saved only where a scheduler step ended on a block boundary, so a unique block mid-prompt gave 0% reuse even behind 15k shared tokens. Cross-agent sharing needs --mamba-cache-mode all (154 MB of state per checkpoint) or block-aligned prompts.
  • The cache saved 60–85% of teammate prompt tokens with the KV cache 1–5% full: prefix reuse buys capacity here, not latency.

13Conclusion

Handing over bytes removes read-rounds efficiently — but read-rounds are a minority of agent work, so total rounds move far less than bytes do

What is deployable

1
Prefer frequency-aware eviction to plain recency — the gain is modest. At 8 KB, GDSF and LFU leave 20.8% and 19.2% of rounds reducible on τ-bench/GAIA against LRU's 15.8%, and 2.6–2.9% against 0.3% on SWE-bench.
2
Spend the budget on prediction, not capacity. On replay, foresight at 8 KB buys the whole unlimited value (38.0 of 38.2) while the best deployable policy gets 58% of it on τ-bench; our harness, whose items are whole files, needs ~32 KB.
3
Key on content and on ranges, never on paths. Agents re-issue a byte-identical read 0.4–2.6% of the time, so an exact-observation key finds almost nothing; matching byte ranges instead of whole reads lifts reducible rounds from 36.4% to 90.9%.

What is not established

  • The live codebase arms are underpowered and the EXPLAIN cell is unreadable. Complete coverage cut rounds once, on W5; on the codebase it is untested, not refuted — ~4× these runs.
  • Reducible rounds on the replay side are computed, not observed.
  • One provider (z.ai glm-5.3-flash) for every live inheritance run, and one codebase for the live arms. HAL cross-task has only 3 groups, and its τ-bench runs share one 50-task set.
  • No accuracy effect was measured on the replay side, and harness accuracy sat at 100% in every arm, so it could only have shown a loss.

What is retired

×
DAG-directed retention. The task graph failed three independent tests: it does not predict which bytes are wanted (slide 4), reaching further does not convert to rounds (slide 6), and as a retention ordering in our harness it never beats LRU by more than a point (slide 7). The artefact worth shipping to an engine is a per-item reuse count, not the board.
×
"Evict what is simplest to recover." Keeping the costliest-to-recover items reaches 10.0% and 1.3% at 8 KB, below LFU, GDSF and S3-FIFO everywhere — recovery cost correlates with size, and the large items are not the reused ones. It must also be measured per item: with a modelled cost, monotone in bytes, the policy silently becomes "keep the largest".
×
lm-evaluation-harness as a source of agentic tasks. It has none and cannot express one. Wrapped as a model endpoint it grades fine — 3/3 on gsm8k through full agentic sessions — but its tasks produced 0 task boards in 15 runs. A benchmark question is one deliverable, and one deliverable cannot be decomposed.

In the live sweep the closure cut reads per successor 60% and mean rounds 25%, which is 58% of the structural ceiling. The arms are working; the ceiling is low.

14Next steps

14
coverage · the study's open question

Power the EXPLAIN arm properly.

  • Roughly 4× these runs, or about 60 successors per arm.

retention unit · the largest measured lever

Implement range-granular content addressing in the injector.

  • Whole read → byte range moves reducible rounds 36.4% → 90.9%, and line granularity adds nothing beyond range.
  • It is the single largest measured lever in the whole study and it is not built.
16
policy · end to end

Test frequency-aware eviction against LRU end to end, capped at 8 KB.

  • none against ancestors under GDSF, LFU and LRU at 8 KB and unlimited — no graph needed; size it for a 3–5 point gap.
  • Needs a genuinely multi-part live workload: SWE-bench instances through mini-swe-agent, or AppWorld.
17
staleness · long trajectories

A staleness-aware policy for long trajectories, where mutation does bite.

  • 7.6–14.3% of a SWE-bench successor's matched bytes are stale, even though stale spans are ~0 in short team runs.
  • Never hand over a span the successor's own base commit has moved past. Ingest AppWorld and Online Mind2Web for a third non-coding regime.
18
harness · the largest payoff on both providers

Make turn-end memory extraction asynchronous or once-per-request.

  • 12–35 s per Lead turn, 20–35% of run wall on both GLM and Qwen, and worse with a thinking model.
  • Also fix the two scheduling faults found this week: spawn_teammate silently refuses a blocked task and loses the successor, and the Lead can shut a team down with an in-progress successor while the run still reports completed.
