1The system under test · orientation for anyone new to this deck

A turn is one Lead activation and a round is one trip through the agent loop — the unit this week is the round, because a round is quantised

The three arms, used in every experiment

nonenothing — the untreated baseline
dagthe reads of its direct predecessors
ancestorsthe reads of its whole transitive closure

The three metrics that carry the story

byte recall
share of the successor's read bytes already held
round recall
share of its rounds that disappear as a result
precision
share of what was sent that was actually used

Quantisation · the mechanism behind every null result

  • A round disappears only when everything it would have fetched is already held — a round issuing two reads needs both.
  • So bytes accumulate smoothly and rounds do not. Byte recall is continuous; round recall is a step.
  • Rounds are what a round costs: a model call is 3.72 s + 0.033 ms per uncached prompt token + 17.44 ms per output token on glm-5.3-flash — prefill is under 2% of it, so a cache buys round count, not prefill.

3Result · does the Lead build a graph at all

The Lead does maintain a dependency graph, and builds it exactly right — last week's 35 empty boards were one adjective in the prompt

Design · edge emission is the dependent variable

  • Nothing may ask the Lead for an edge. No prompt names update_task, addBlockedBy, "dependency" or "blocked"; the harness is unmodified. census_workloads.py --check-prompts refuses to run otherwise.
  • Six categories × {DEP, FLAT} × reps on two providers. DEP work genuinely has stages; FLAT genuinely does not — the specificity control.
  • 44 usable runs (26 DeepSeek, 18 glm). Each DEP workload ships a hand-authored reference graph.

One word explains the 35 empty boards

Every earlier "natural" workload literally began "Create three independent task-board items" (latency_workloads.py:40, redundancy_workloads.py:37, 50, 62, 76). Those 59 runs — 35 with a board, 133 tasks, 0 edges, 0 update_task calls — were never evidence about whether a Lead maintains a graph.

What this repairs, and what it does not

  • Repairs the premise. Last week's note reported the Lead "does not emit edges unless the prompt names the mechanism". These prompts are L0 by that report's own definition and produce complete graphs.
  • The board is available early enough to be useful — the whole graph exists before any work starts, so a retention directive could be computed at spawn time.
  • It does not resurrect DAG-directed retention. Slides 4, 7 and 8 kill the graph on its merits, not on its absence.
  • Cross-provider. Identical board shape on both; cost differs 4× — DeepSeek 118 s mean per DEP run against glm's 488 s. That is provider and model together, not a model property.
  • The work is right, not just the graph. census_score.py: 10 of 10 attempted sandboxes passed, including REFACTOR-DEP, whose tests only go green if all 7 call sites moved.

One adjective moved 35 runs. Any future claim about what Leads do with the board must quote the prompt that produced it.

4Result · is the edge the right key

The edge predicts the successor's reads only because it echoes the task text — and broadcasting every earlier read beats it, not narrowly

The placebo · W4 is W1's text word for word, edges wired to the wrong branch

  • In aligned boards (W1, W2, W5) an edge covers +46 points more of the successor's read bytes than a non-adjacent control with the same successor — 95% CI [+0.27, +0.68], 5 of 5 runs positive — and +37 points of removable rounds.
  • In the placebo the edges cover 0.0% of their successors' bytes while the matched controls cover everything: a delta of exactly −1.0 in both reps.
  • So the signal is in the task text, not in the graph. Pooling all five workloads gives a flat +0.105, p = 0.68 — an artefact of mixing the placebo in, and it should not be quoted.

Sending 3.5× too much costs 3.9 s of prefill and still buys 63 s of rounds removed. At 0.033 ms per uncached token, being wrong is cheap; being incomplete is not (slide 6).

Two structural facts about the boards

  • Boards are small, wide and shallow — median 5 tasks, 3 edges, 2 layers, and only 23% of tasks have any predecessor at all (163 tasks over 32 runs).
  • Across 35 successors with 2.5 predecessors each, 67% of predecessor links cross agents and 33 of 35 successors are missing something. A join waits for its slowest predecessor, so the first one's output is 153 s stale by the time the join starts.
  • Intra-agent reuse is real but partial: the 6 of 13 successors that landed on an agent that had already done a predecessor read 3.7 files and got 44.7% of bytes from them; the 7 that landed fresh read more (5.7) and got less (29.9%).

Recall is the whole game; precision is nearly free to give up. The dependency graph is the wrong key.

What real project work does that benchmarks do not

  • Codebase tasks produce boards every time: 20 of 20 runs, 11 of 20 at depth ≥ 3. On lm-evaluation-harness, 0 of 15 runs created a task at all — not merely no edges, no board — including one with a 1.59 MB document attached.
  • lm-eval has 221 task families, all static; its OutputType admits no tool or environment step, and SWE-bench, GAIA, τ-bench, BFCL, AppWorld and WebArena are all absent. Wrapped as a model endpoint it grades fine — 3/3 on gsm8k — which is exactly the point: a benchmark question is one deliverable, and one deliverable cannot be decomposed.
  • The two shapes differ: comprehension work fans in, modification work chains, and only the chaining shape reaches the depth where dag and ancestors can differ at all.
  • Waste is model-dependent — within-trajectory round recall on the identical 50 τ-bench tasks runs gemini-2.0-flash 20.8% to deepseek-v3 5.2%, a 4× spread on the same work.

If this line of work needs a target workload, it is τ-bench-shaped, not SWE-bench-shaped.

What did not change, and what it means for KV sharing

  • Quality is identical — 24/24 coding, 24/24 math, FQA coverage 0.98 ± 0.02 — at 1.8–3.4× the wall time. Prefill is 1–6% of a call and decode 90–99% on both.
  • Rounds per task are model-independent at this scale: 7 / 4 / 3 teammate rounds, 11–14 lead rounds per team run, and the redundancy pattern is unchanged. The wall-time gap is decode speed times output length.
  • Two vLLM facts for a KV-sharing design. Cache blocks are 784 tokens on this hybrid model, and under --mamba-cache-mode align a foreign request can only resume from a checkpoint position — a unique block mid-prompt gave 0% reuse even behind 15k tokens of shared prefix. Cross-agent sharing needs --mamba-cache-mode all (154 MB of state per checkpoint) or block-aligned prompts.
  • Turn-end memory extraction is still the largest non-agent cost — 12–35 s per lead turn, 20–35% of run wall, and worse with a thinking model. Making it asynchronous or once-per-request is the harness change with the largest payoff on both providers.

11Conclusion

Handing over the bytes removes read-rounds efficiently — and read-rounds are a minority of what an agent does, so total rounds move far less than the byte numbers suggest

What is deployable

1
Rank by reuse frequency, not recency. At 8 KB, LFU removes 21.9% of rounds on τ-bench/GAIA and 4.0% on SWE-bench; LRU removes 3.0% and 0.0%. Recency truncation and last-N-round windows are the worst deployable family tested.
2
Spend the budget on prediction, not capacity. With perfect foresight 8 KB already buys the entire unlimited-budget value — 38.0 of 38.2, and 4.2 of 4.3. Every gap is a prediction failure.
3
Key on content and on ranges, never on paths. An exact-observation key finds almost nothing — agents re-issue a byte-identical read 0.4–2.6% of the time — while 86% of the bytes sit in files a predecessor already opened.
4
Widen the pool before tuning the policy. Removable rounds scale 13% → 23% → 36% as the pool grows 8 → 16 → 32 earlier tasks, roughly linear in the log.
5
Do not partition the pool per agent. Size-matched, inheriting from a different model's runs is as good as inheriting from your own, within ~2 points at every pool size.

What is retired

×
DAG-directed retention. The task graph failed a third independent test: as a priority ordering under budget it sits near the bottom of both policy tables — 0.7% on SWE at 8 KB. It does not predict which bytes are wanted, reaching further does not convert to rounds, and it is useless as an ordering. The artefact worth shipping to an engine is a per-item reuse count, not the board.
×
"Evict what is simplest to recover." Keeping the costliest-to-recover items reaches 11.1% and 2.3% at 8 KB, below LFU, GDSF and S3-FIFO everywhere — recovery cost correlates with size, and the large items are not the reused ones. It must also be measured per item: with a modelled cost, monotone in bytes, the policy silently becomes "keep the largest".
×
lm-evaluation-harness as a source of agentic tasks. It has none and cannot express one. Wrapped as a model endpoint it grades fine — 3/3 on gsm8k through full agentic sessions — but its tasks produced 0 task boards in 15 runs. A benchmark question is one deliverable, and one deliverable cannot be decomposed.

What is not established

  • The live arms are underpowered and the EXPLAIN cell is unreadable. Coverage as the lever is untested, not refuted — it needs roughly 4× these runs.
  • Rounds removed are counterfactual on the replay side, computed rather than observed.
  • One provider (z.ai glm-5.3-flash) for everything live, and one codebase for the live arms. HAL cross-task has only 3 groups, and its τ-bench runs share one 50-task set.
  • No accuracy effect was measured on the replay side — though the synthetic study found partial coverage hurts accuracy, 99% → 83%.

In the live sweep the closure cut reads per successor 60% and mean rounds 25%, which is 58% of the structural ceiling. The arms are working; the ceiling is low.
