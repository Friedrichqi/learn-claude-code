# Stage 3 — Critique of stage2_tests.md

## Overall assessment

The ten cases map faithfully onto the runtime behaviours in stage1_notes.md: every glossary area (loop control, compaction, background, cron, recovery, permissions/hooks, plan gates, task board, tracing) is exercised, and the expected results cite the exact mechanisms and constants (head 3 + tail 46, 512000 chars, 500 ms ×2 cap 32 s, 0600 mode). The two systemic flaws are (a) **over-bundling** — cases 3, 5, 6, and 10 each pack three to six independent behaviours into one pass/fail verdict, so a failure cannot be attributed to a specific behaviour without re-running the case piecemeal; and (b) **unactionable or flaky setups** — several "Action" lines do not specify how the precondition is produced (stubbed model responses, error injection, size growth), and some depend on second-scale timing that will not reproduce reliably. No case states how the model's output is forced, which affects cases 1 and 6 most.

## The three weakest cases

**Case 3 — CONTEXT_LIMIT guard on full summarization.** It bundles three unrelated behaviours (the size-guarded `compact_history`, the `compact` tool special case, and `reactive_compact`'s once-per-turn rule) into one case, so any of three failures produces the same red flag. Its setup is also the most expensive in the plan: driving the estimate above 512000 chars means materializing roughly half a megabyte of JSON-serialized context per attempt, and the "at most once per turn" assertion requires forcing *repeated* prompt-too-long errors inside a single turn, which the plan gives no mechanism for. Fix: split into three cases; test the 512000-char gate with a stubbed `estimate_size` rather than real payload growth, and verify the once-per-turn cap with an injected error loop.

**Case 5 — Cron scheduling, dedup, durability, and one-shot delivery.** The setup is internally contradictory as written: a 5-field cron expression cannot "fire twice in the same minute" — the per-minute `_last_fired` dedup marker exists precisely to stop multiple one-second scheduler ticks within the same minute from double-enqueuing one due job. A tester following the action literally would either build an impossible schedule or accidentally test tick-timing race behavior. On top of that, the case stacks two further scenarios (durable reload from `.scheduled_tasks.json` across a restart, and one-shot `pending_delivery` acknowledgement), one of which requires restarting the harness process mid-test. Fix: separate the dedup probe (two scheduler ticks inside one minute, deterministic if the tick interval is controllable), the restart/reload probe, and the one-shot delivery probe into independent cases.

**Case 6 — Model-call error recovery paths.** Four distinct recovery strategies (429/529 retry, fallback after two consecutive 529s, `max_tokens` escalation plus continuations, prompt-too-long compaction) share one verdict, and two of its assertions are practically unverifiable as stated: the backoff schedule includes up to 25% jitter, but a run exposes at most two sleep intervals (3 attempts), from which no timing property — the 32 s cap, the ×2 growth, the jitter bound — can be demonstrated; and distinguishing a 529-triggered fallback switch from any other model change requires error injection the plan never specifies. Fix: one case per recovery path; assert retry *counts* and observable state changes (fallback model id, escalation to 16000, one `reactive_compact`) rather than sleep durations, and verify backoff constants by inspecting injected sleep calls, not wall-clock timing.

## Close runners-up

Case 2's action ("grow context until each layer's condition is met") names no per-layer thresholds, so the tester cannot know when `snip_compact` vs `micro_compact` will fire or craft a history shape that exercises the head-3/tail-46 and "3 newest" rules in one run. Case 10 stitches six behaviours (import defaults, env-driven recorder, envelope, spans, contextvars attribution, stats validation) into one case, though each part is at least cheaply and directly observable.

## What holds up well

Cases 1, 7, and 8 are the strongest: single-question setups, binary observable outcomes tied to named trace events and hook strings, and explicit coverage of the fail-closed branches (async permission turns, plan-gate re-arm on new assignment). Case 9's dependency-edge rejections are crisp; only its concurrent claim/update step is inherently nondeterministic and would be better asserted as "no lost update or duplicate owner across N trials" than as a single run.
