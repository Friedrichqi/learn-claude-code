## Summary: shift and EPIC after s15's snip

**e4: divergence-triggered full recomputation (MiniMax-M2.7, all three benchmarks, 2026-10-15 to 17).**
- **Design.**
  - The user asked how urgent a full recomputation is after shift+EPIC.
  - A new arm, `epic128rjsd` (shift+EPIC+recompute@JSD), compares the first-token distributions under the
    original cache (every row in its first-ever version) and under the after-EPIC cache at every curated call.
    It recomputes everything after the never-edited head with probability JSD.
  - 7 arms; the profile's first 30 tasks per benchmark × 3 replicates.
  - Pre-registered (`runs/epic/prereg_e4.md`); exploratory, intervals only.
- **Accuracy, against the control** (mean of baseline and prefix_aa per block; 90 % t-intervals over 30 task means):

  | arm | SWE-bench Verified | SWE-bench Pro | Terminal-Bench |

  The JSD arm's figures include its decisions' own cost (the original-cache forward) and its recomputations.
- **The decisions.**
  - 6,414 in all, none failed.
  - Recompute rate: SWE-bench Verified 5.5%; SWE-bench Pro 5.3%; Terminal-Bench 7.0%.
  - Mean JSD: SWE-bench Verified 0.0531; SWE-bench Pro 0.0461; Terminal-Bench 0.0733.
  - **Noise floor.** Two probes of the same after-EPIC cache differ by a mean JSD of 0.0354; 0.0306; 0.0527. The server is not batch-invariant, so much of the decision metric is noise.
  - **It still tracks the real error.** Spearman ρ between JSD and the shadow probe's first-token KL (fresh
    recompute vs after-EPIC) on the same calls: SWE-bench Verified +0.529 [+0.416, +0.625] (n 132); SWE-bench Pro +0.599 [+0.443, +0.720] (n 61); Terminal-Bench +0.442 [+0.298, +0.567] (n 99). TVD, EXP and KL track it almost identically.
  - A decision took 2.7 s; 5.2 s; 9.3 s on average, in emulation.
- **Machinery.**
  - Every gate passed in all 9 benchmark-replicates, except E-0 on Terminal-Bench (95.7–96.6 %), flagged
    above its 90 % stop, the same template limit as in e2.
  - No preemptions; no fallbacks.
  - All 270 blocks valid; no rerun needed.
  - 141.7 GPU-h, the pilot included, against a cap of 300.
- **Verification.** Every cell above and in the e4 tables is generated by `report/e4_report.py`.
  - `report/e4_verify.py` recomputed all 396 values from the raw files with its own code:
    0 mismatches, 0 broken invariants.
  - The same report code reproduces e3's 81 published cells.
  - Spot traces: `runs/epic/e4_spot_traces.md`.

**Setup.**
- After about 25 turns, s15's rolling 50-message snip (`snip_compact`) rewrites the middle of the history on every
  call, so the prefix cache stops helping: 82–97 % of re-prefilled tokens come after a snip (section 3).
- This round reuses the KV of the messages that survive the snip:
  - `shift` re-rotates them to their new positions;
  - `epic128` also recomputes the first 128 tokens after every edit;
  - `epic128a1` also recomputes rows that have survived more than one edit.
- Coverage:
  - MiniMax-M2.7:
    - e1: confirmatory, 90 paired blocks per arm on SWE-bench Verified;
    - e2: exploratory, on SWE-bench Pro and Terminal-Bench, 30 blocks per arm, added at the user's request;
    - **e3: pre-registered, 300 paired blocks per arm on 100 new SWE-bench Verified tasks**, the user's follow-up
      question;
  - Qwen3.8-27B: exploratory, a 10-trajectory replay and a 5-task live pilot;
  - DeepSeek-V4-Flash: design and selftests.

**e3: the pre-registered answer to "does EPIC cost accuracy?" (MiniMax-M2.7, SWE-bench Verified).**
- **Design.**
  - 100 tasks that neither e1 nor the profile used, 3 replicates, so 300 paired blocks per arm.
  - Fixed in `runs/epic/prereg_e3.md` before the first trial: the hypotheses, sample, tests and stopping rule.
  - The agent timeout was doubled for every arm.
  - Control = the mean of prefix and prefix_aa in each block.
  - Differences are in success rate (0.05 = 5 points), with 90 % t-intervals over the 100 task means, i.e.
    one-sided 95 % bounds.

  | arm | pass@1 | difference from the control | 90 % interval | result |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | prefix | 0.730 | | | |
  | prefix_aa | 0.750 | +0.020 against prefix (A/A) | [−0.017, +0.057] | covers 0: the check passes |
  | epic128 | 0.730 | −0.010 | [−0.043, +0.023] | **non-inferior at a 5-point margin (H1, primary)** |
  | epic128a1 | 0.740 | −0.000 | [−0.033, +0.033] | **not shown to be worse (H2)** |
  | epic128a4 | 0.757 | +0.017 | [−0.014, +0.047] | exploratory |

- **H1 holds.** With 95 % confidence, epic128 costs less than 5 points: its lower bound is −4.3 points.
- **H2 does not.** The epic128a1 drop of about 7 points in e1 and e2 did not replicate on new tasks.
  - Pooled over e1, e2 and e3 (descriptive), epic128a1 shrinks to −0.023 [−0.050, +0.004].
  - Pooled the same way, epic128 is −0.008 [−0.036, +0.020].
  - The looser age cap, epic128a4, is not worse either.
- **No looping:**
  - 44–48 lead calls per run in every arm;
  - 1.0–1.7 % of runs at the 250-call budget;
  - no agent timeouts.
- **Prefill.** The share of the prompt computed falls from 47.7 % (prefix) to:
  - 4.3 % for epic128, i.e. 6,937 → 634 tokens per call, −91 %;
  - 11.0 % for epic128a4 (−77 %);
  - 24.6 % for epic128a1 (−49 %).
- **Time.** Modeled LLM time per run is 239 s for prefix, 217 s for epic128 (−9 %) and 210 s for epic128a4. Job
  time is 0.90–1.00× prefix, because decode dominates (`latency_minimax-m2.7-epic_swe_e3.md`).
- **Machinery.** Every gate passed in every replicate:
  - E-0 99.4–99.6 %;
  - no preemptions and no fallbacks;
  - 17 resets in 41,380 EPIC-arm calls, all of them replies longer than the 4,096-token decode reserve;
  - one server record per call; consistent ranks; no session left.
- **One deviation.** The interim check stopped the run falsely at 02:01 UTC, because of a read-order race in the
  gates script itself. It was fixed and tested, and the run resumed at 02:25 UTC. The design was unchanged, and no
  outcome had been looked at.
- **Cost:** 72 GPU-h of the approved ~86.

**Accuracy, MiniMax-M2.7, e1** (e3 above supersedes these results for epic128 and epic128a1).

The control is the mean of prefix (0.656) and prefix_aa (0.689). Differences from it, with 90 % t-intervals over the
30 task means:

| arm | difference | 90 % interval |
|---|---|---|
| epic128 | +0.017 | [−0.043, +0.076] |
| shift | −0.050 | [−0.129, +0.029] |
| epic128a1 | −0.061 | [−0.130, +0.007] |
| prefix_aa vs prefix (A/A) | +0.033 | [−0.016, +0.083] |

- The pre-registered test (fixed order epic128a1, epic128, shift; margin −0.10) stopped at epic128a1, whose lower
  bound missed the margin by 0.03. So **no arm is formally declared non-inferior**.
- Outside that sequence, epic128 would pass and shift would not. All three point estimates lie within the A/A noise.
- No looping: 48–53 lead calls per run in every arm, and 2–4 % of runs at the 250-call budget. That differs from
  topic 16's Qwen3-32B.

**SWE-bench Pro and Terminal-Bench, MiniMax-M2.7 (phase e2).**
- **Design:** exploratory, one replicate of 30 blocks per arm, the agent timeout doubled for every arm. The
  budget allowed no second replicate.
- **Pass@1 by arm** (the control is the mean of prefix and prefix_aa; baseline = the profile's runs on the same tasks):

  | | baseline | prefix | prefix_aa | shift | epic128 | epic128a1 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | SWE-bench Pro | 0.867 | 0.833 | 0.867 | 0.833 | 0.767 | 0.767 |
  | Terminal-Bench | 0.478 | 0.633 | 0.333 | 0.500 | 0.633 | 0.533 |

- **SWE-bench Pro, which sits near its ceiling.**
  - Differences from the control: shift −0.017 [−0.093, +0.059], epic128 −0.083 [−0.206, +0.039], epic128a1 −0.083
    [−0.155, −0.012]. Only epic128a1's interval excludes zero, and only just; without the one block that had a
    fallback it becomes [−0.139, +0.001].
  - On SWE-bench Verified epic128a1 also had the lowest point estimate (−0.061), although it has the best fidelity.
    That was worth a look, but it was not established. e3 then tested it on 100 new tasks and found no drop:
    −0.000 [−0.033, +0.033].
  - The A/A difference was +0.033 [−0.066, +0.133].
- **Terminal-Bench: no accuracy conclusion.**
  - The two identical control arms differ by −0.300 [−0.485, −0.115] (13 agent timeouts against 6). The cause is
    trajectory divergence plus a time limit that binds hard on the emulating server, where a call takes about 20 s.
  - The arms' differences from the control (+0.017 to +0.150) are inside that noise.
- **Prefill and fidelity carry over.** Prompt tokens computed per call:

  | | prefix | shift | epic128 | epic128a1 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | SWE-bench Pro | 7,519 | 884 (−88 %) | 964 (−87 %) | 3,615 (−52 %) |
  | Terminal-Bench | 7,952 | 1,508 (−81 %) | 752 (−91 %) | 4,410 (−45 %) |

  - First-token KL against a fresh recompute: shift 0.045 on Pro and 0.104 on Terminal-Bench, against 0.040 on
    SWE-bench Verified; epic128a1 0.015–0.020. Reply top-1 agreement is 96.5–98.7 %.
  - The modeled job time again changes little: 0.91–1.09× prefix on Pro.
- **Machinery.**
  - Pro passed every gate (E-0 99.4 %).
  - Terminal-Bench passed every gate except E-0, which was 94.2 %. Its long reasoning hits the template's
    `\n\n\n` limit far more often.
  - Terminal-Bench also exhausted the 640K-row host pool once, at 15:20–15:23 UTC: 10 post-snip fallbacks.
  - 23 decode-overflow resets came from replies longer than the 4,096-token reserve.

**What reuse does to the model.**
- **MiniMax live shadow probes.**
  - First-token KL: 0.017 (epic128a1) to 0.040 (shift). Reply KL: 0.009–0.021. Reply top-1 agreement: 97–98 %.
  - The K deviation of reused rows grows with the number of edits they have survived: from 0.02 (none) to
    0.23–0.25 (16 or more) for shift and epic128. The age cap keeps it at or below 0.1.
- **Qwen3.8 replay**, on the same recorded prompts in every arm (522 calls per arm). First-token KL against prefix:
  - prefix_aa (noise floor) 0.006, epic128a1 0.007, epic128 0.017, shift 0.018;
  - for shift and epic128 it grows from 0.007 before the first snip to 0.032–0.035 after 20 or more snips.
- **Qwen3.8 pilot**, 5 tasks: prefix 5/5, epic128 4/5, epic128a1 4/5, shift 3/5. The EPIC arms ran about 50 % more
  lead calls (118–128 against 78). That hints at topic 16's looping effect, but 5 blocks cannot resolve it.
- **DeepSeek-V4-Flash.** The port works mechanically. Its run-to-run noise is large (7–10 % in recomputed attention
  inputs), so the selftests cannot show exactness below it.

**Performance.**
- **Prefill.**
  - MiniMax: prompt tokens computed per call fall from 7,552 (prefix, unbounded cache) to 605 (shift) and 702
    (epic128), which is −92 %; epic128a1 computes 3,835 (−49 %).
  - Qwen3.8 replay: the computed share falls from 66.5 % of the prompt to 5.7–6.1 %; epic128a1 computes 35 %.
- **Time.** Decode (about 20 ms per output token, about 200 output tokens per call) and fixed overhead dominate
  MiniMax's lead-call time on SWE-bench.
  - So the modeled job time changes by only −6 % (epic128) to +3 % (shift, epic128a1).
  - This is a lower-bound model, fitted on the baseline's measured calls (median absolute error 29 %).
  - On a prefill-bound server, cutting 90 % of the prefill would raise throughput far more than one run's latency
    suggests. The emulating server was prefill-bound at about 15K prompt tokens/s with 20 sessions. This round
    did not measure it: emulation computes every token.
  - Qwen3.8 and DeepSeek-V4 save no compute in this live form. The hybrid's DeltaNet layers recompute every token,
    and DeepSeek-V4's pooled and indexer caches are rebuilt from stored inputs.

**The machinery itself.**
- MiniMax e1, every gate in every replicate:
  - no preemptions, no fallbacks, no post-snip resets;
  - one server record per tagged call (22,814);
  - identical overrides on all 4 ranks;
  - append-only round-trip (E-0) at 99.2–99.4 %.
- Qwen3.8: pooled E-0 of 99.0 %. Its misses come from non-canonical tokenization of sampled text. Every other gate
  passed.

**Deviations** (details in each part):
- e3: a false interim stop caused by the gates script's read order; fixed, tested and resumed (deviation 1 in the
  e3 notes);
- after pilot ep1: a KV watermark and more KV memory, against preemptions;
- one-task chunks, run as a forward and a reverse batch;
- the agent timeout binds earlier for the EPIC arms on the emulating server (a sensitivity analysis is reported);
- Qwen3.8's reply-level probe metrics are invalid on 3 of 4 instances (fixed during the stage; the valid subset is
  reported).
