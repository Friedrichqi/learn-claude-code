**Policies.** Value = the share of the total EPIC-128 refresh value (sum of v over the edited calls, 13.57) a policy removes. A refreshed call is served as a full re-prefill from its first non-fresh row: refresh tokens = EPIC-128's reused rows S at an edit call (everything else behind the first reused row is computed by EPIC anyway), the whole stale span since the last edit at an append-only call. The refreshed call is credited its refresh value v (the pre-registered value of a call is capped at v, and refreshing every call is 100 %); 'exact credit' counts its whole KL instead (a re-prefill leaves KL ~ 0), i.e. also the one-step residual, which is not refresh value (it can exceed 100 %). After a refresh no reused row is older than the edits since: the first edit after it is the measured one-step arm and the append-only calls before that are fresh (measured); later calls use the age model: M1 (main), 'measured only' (no model: those calls get nothing), isotonic, M2 (KV load). Cost = (EPIC-128's computed tokens + refresh tokens) / (P - E). Intervals: (a), (b) run bootstrap with the age model refit per replicate (200 replicates); (c), (c') [5 %, 95 %] over the 30 held-out prediction splits (median shown); 'passes in' = the share of replicates (splits) in which the policy passes D2. An oracle that knew each call's v would pass (89% at 0.33): the value is concentrated enough, but not predictable enough from what the server knows before the call.

For the design:
- **Age is the lever.** Per-row K deviation grows with the edits a row survived (EPIC-128: 0.020 after one edit, 0.032 after 4-7, 0.051 after 8-15, 0.092 after 16+; one-step 0.017); rows that survived >= 8 edits are 29% of the stale rows but carry 60% of their excess. A partial refresh should recompute the oldest surviving rows: in s15's chronological history they open the moved tail, right behind the head, so a prefix-order refresh of the tail's first part reaches them first (structure of the snip, not measured here).
- **Offset is not.** Rows within 512 tokens of a run head deviate more (0.088 vs 0.033 beyond, behind EPIC's 128-token head), but 80% of the reused rows' excess lies >= 512 tokens from any head: a longer head (larger k) cannot reach it.
- **Layers 40-63 carry 78% of the excess** (layers 0-31: 14%). A refresh still recomputes every layer of a row (a deep layer's KV depends on the same row's shallow layers), so depth does not cut its cost; a drift probe can read the deep layers only.
